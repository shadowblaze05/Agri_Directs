CHAPTER IV
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

4.1 Introduction

This chapter presents the Agri-Direct system results in relation to the
study objectives concerning the centralized agricultural supply-chain
platform, market intelligence, and algorithm-based decision support. The
discussion is limited to the features and data sources relevant to these
objectives.

4.2 Centralized Agricultural Supply-Chain Platform

Agri-Direct provides a shared platform for managing agricultural
inventory and product listings, coordinating marketplace transactions,
and communicating between users. The system uses standard user accounts
rather than separate farmer and buyer account types. A user's role as a
seller or buyer depends on their activity in a transaction.

Users can record harvest quantities and use eligible inventory to create
Marketplace listings. Listings present information such as crop, amount,
price, unit, seller, and location. Users can browse available produce,
review listing details, and manage their own listings. The system checks
the requested listing quantity against available inventory when a user
creates a listing.

The Marketplace supports purchases and trades for available listings.
Users can follow their own purchases and sales. Before processing a
purchase, the system checks the requested quantity and listing
availability. A partial purchase reduces the amount remaining; a fully
purchased listing is updated accordingly.

Direct messaging allows users to communicate within the platform. Users
can select contacts, review conversation history, and send messages.
These communication features are available alongside listings and
transactions.

Discussion

The inventory, Marketplace, transaction, and messaging features bring
product management, transactions, and communication together in one
platform. They support the first study objective by allowing users to
record and list agricultural products, transact with other users, and
communicate directly. The system has a shared user role, so references to
farmers and buyers describe transaction activities rather than separate
account types.

4.3 Market Intelligence

Agri-Direct includes inventory-based price indicators and supply-balance
analysis. The results are calculated from records available in the
application and should be interpreted within the limits of those data.

4.3.1 Price Monitoring

The price-monitoring feature presents a modeled indicator derived from
stored inventory quantities. It is a relative price signal and not a
currency price quote. The current implementation does not retrieve
external live commodity prices or use a verified historical market-price
series. Therefore, it does not establish actual market prices or
historical price trends from external market data.

Discussion

The price indicator provides an inventory-derived signal for review
alongside supply information. Because it is not based on externally
verified historical and current market prices, the result should not be
presented as a measured market-price trend. This limitation narrows the
extent to which the implemented feature addresses the price-monitoring
part of the second objective.

4.3.2 Oversupply and Undersupply Detection

The supply-balance feature groups recorded inventory quantities by crop
and compares them with dynamically calculated thresholds. Based on the
comparison, the system classifies supply conditions such as balanced,
undersupply, critical shortage, high supply, or oversupply. The
associated alerts describe the detected condition.

Discussion

The supply-balance results identify potential imbalances in the
inventory recorded by the system. The classifications are based on
system quantities and thresholds; they do not represent a verified
measurement of total supply across a geographic market. This feature
supports the undersupply and oversupply detection part of the second
objective, subject to the available data.

4.4 Algorithm-Based Decision Support

Agri-Direct provides crop recommendations and quantity forecasts using
statistical and rule-based methods. These outputs are decision-support
estimates based on the information available to the system; they should
not be interpreted as guarantees of crop performance or future demand.

4.4.1 Crop Recommendation

The crop-recommendation service ranks crop options through a
multi-criteria process. It uses five criteria: demand strength, supply
stability, seasonal fit, price opportunity, and risk balance. The
Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) derives weights from a fixed pairwise
comparison matrix, and the Technique for Order Preference by Similarity
to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) calculates scores used to rank the options.

The criteria values are derived from inventory and forecast information
available to the application. In particular, demand, seasonality, and
price opportunity are represented by inventory-derived proxies rather
than dedicated historical transaction-demand records, an independently
verified crop-season calendar, or verified market-price data. The
rankings are therefore recommendations based on the implemented inputs
and criteria, not agronomic guarantees or promises of profitability.

Discussion

The AHP/TOPSIS process produces ranked crop suggestions from the
implemented criteria and available data. This supports the crop
recommendation part of the third objective. The recommendations'
relevance is limited by the use of inventory-derived proxies for some
factors named in the objective.

4.4.2 Demand Forecasting

The demand-forecasting service produces a one-step quantity estimate
from available positive inventory observations. When the data contain
sufficient variation, the service attempts an ARIMA(1,0,0) forecast. If
there is insufficient data or the model fit fails, it uses a linear
fallback where possible; when the data are insufficient, it reports
that condition. Forecast quantities are constrained to non-negative
values.

The forecast observations are inventory quantities, not a dedicated
historical transaction-demand dataset. The output should therefore be
described as an inventory-derived quantity estimate rather than a
validated measure of future buyer demand. Forecast accuracy has not
been established against actual future observations.

Discussion

The forecasting service provides an algorithm-based quantity estimate
from recorded inventory history and supports the demand-forecasting part
of the third objective. Its input data differ from historical
transaction records, and the estimate should not be treated as validated
demand. Claims about forecast accuracy require comparison with actual
observations.

4.5 Summary of Results in Relation to the Objectives

The results relevant to each objective are summarized below:

- Objective 1: Inventory and product listings, purchases and trades, and
  direct messaging provide product management, transaction, and
  communication functions in one platform. Accounts use a shared
  standard-user role.
- Objective 2: Inventory-based price indicators and crop supply
  classifications provide modeled price signals and potential
  supply-imbalance alerts. Price signals are not sourced from verified
  external market-price data.
- Objective 3: AHP/TOPSIS produces ranked crop suggestions, and the
  forecasting service produces inventory-derived quantity estimates.
  Some recommendation factors are proxies, and forecasts use inventory
  rather than historical transaction-demand data.

Overall, Agri-Direct brings together product management, marketplace
transactions, and direct communication, and provides inventory-based
market signals and algorithm-based decision-support outputs. The market
intelligence and decision-support results should be interpreted in
light of their data sources: price monitoring uses an inventory-derived
indicator, and recommendations and forecasts rely on available
inventory and proxy values rather than verified external market prices
or a dedicated historical transaction-demand dataset.

CHAPTER IV
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

4.1 Introduction

This chapter presents the developed Agri-Direct system and discusses its
modules in relation to the four specific objectives of the study. The
discussion follows the system from the public landing page, through account
access and the user-facing functions, to the administrator console. The
implemented system provides marketplace and inventory functions,
transactions, direct messaging, inventory-based market analysis, crop
recommendations, demand forecasting, seller ratings, and a Digital
Agricultural Knowledge Hub.

The chapter also provides spaces for functional testing and system
evaluation results. No formal user evaluation data has been collected or
provided at this time; therefore, the relevant tables, figures, and
interpretations are intentionally left as placeholders. No ratings,
respondent counts, test results, or accuracy claims are fabricated.

Implementation note: Agri-Direct currently uses standard user and admin
roles rather than separate farmer and buyer account types. A standard user
can perform user functions such as listing products or buying from another
user. Where this chapter uses "farmer" or "buyer," it refers to the user's
activity in a transaction, not a separate account role or dashboard.


4.2 Developed Agri-Direct System

Agri-Direct is a web-based agricultural marketplace and decision-support
platform. It brings together a public information page, authenticated user
functions, data-driven agricultural modules, communication features, and
administrator workspaces. The system is intended to help users organize
harvest and product information, find and transact agricultural listings,
and review information derived from records stored in the platform.

The user flow starts at the public landing page. Visitors can review the
platform information and proceed to Login or Register. Once authenticated,
users can access the signed-in Home page, Dashboard, Marketplace,
Knowledge Hub, Messages, Notifications, and Profile. An administrator
account has additional navigation to the Admin Console and its management
workspaces.

4.3 User Registration and Authentication

The registration and authentication module controls access to Agri-Direct.
The Register page collects account information and validates required
credentials, username format and length, password length, password
confirmation, and duplicate usernames or email addresses. Successful
registration creates a standard user account. The Login page accepts a
username or email address and password. Valid credentials establish a
session; invalid or incomplete credentials produce a message and do not
authenticate the user.

The account role also controls access to administrative functions. Standard
users cannot access the Admin Console, while accounts with the admin role
can use the additional management workspaces. This supports the role-based
access boundary required for the system's administrative functions.

4.4 Farmer and Buyer User Functions

Agri-Direct provides a shared signed-in experience rather than separate
farmer and buyer dashboards. A user's activity determines whether they
function as a seller/farmer or buyer in a particular transaction. The
signed-in Home page displays recent Agricultural Updates, featured
Marketplace listings, a Marketplace overview, and a Market Brief derived
from available harvest data. Navigation links provide access to the
Dashboard, harvest upload, Marketplace, Knowledge Hub, Messages,
Notifications, and Profile.

The Dashboard presents an overview of recorded inventory. It includes
headline figures such as total harvest, top crop, crop-type count, and
location count, along with recent inventory and period-based statistics.
The dashboard and related pages provide users with different views of
harvest data, including total harvest, top crop, crop types, and locations.

4.5 Agricultural Marketplace and Product Management

The Marketplace is the central location for browsing agricultural product
listings. Available listing information can include crop name, amount,
price, unit, seller, location, description, expiry, and seller-rating
details. Users can search and filter listings and open product details.

A user can create a listing from eligible inventory they own. The listing
form validates the crop, available quantity, amount, and price before
creating an available listing. If the user does not have eligible
inventory, the system directs them to record harvest data before listing
produce. The user can review and manage their own listings through the My
Listings page.

The Marketplace and product management functions directly support Specific

4.6 Inventory Management

The inventory module allows users to record harvest data and review
agricultural quantities stored in the system. A harvest can be entered
manually or imported from a CSV file. Manual entries identify a crop and
quantity, and may include a received date and supporting evidence. CSV
imports process crop name, quantity, and an optional date. Valid rows are
added to the user's inventory; invalid rows are reported rather than being
treated as successful records.

The Dashboard uses inventory records for its totals and crop summaries.
Users can review inventory grouped by crop and can update or remove records
subject to ownership rules. Administrators have separate tools for
searching and reviewing inventory records. Some harvest records can be
flagged for manual review when submitted evidence or location information
is incomplete. The location check is a proximity indicator; it does not
prove identity, land ownership, or crop authenticity.

Inventory is a core data source for the Marketplace, Dashboard, and

4.7 Transaction Management

The transaction functions allow users to buy or trade for an available
Marketplace listing and follow their own sales or purchases. A buyer can
select a quantity, and the system checks listing availability and the
requested amount before processing the purchase. A partial purchase
reduces the remaining listing amount; a fully purchased listing changes
its status. The trade workflow allows an eligible user to offer a crop
they own in exchange for a listed product. Transaction pages provide
users with information about listing, purchase, and order status. The
system also supports transaction completion and buyer ratings where the
transaction is eligible.

centralized agricultural platform.

4.9 Market Intelligence Module

The Market Intelligence module provides analytical information derived
from agricultural inventory records. Its implemented functions include
inventory-based price monitoring, supply-balance analysis, crop
recommendations, and demand forecasting. Detailed Market Intelligence
pages are restricted to administrator accounts in the current system;
the signed-in Home page also displays a shorter inventory-derived Market
Brief to users.

The present implementation does not connect to an external live
commodity-price service. Accordingly, references to "real-time market
price monitoring" in the study objective should be interpreted carefully:
the current module calculates an inventory-based price index or price
signal when system data is analyzed; it does not retrieve verified,
real-time market prices from an external source. This limitation should
be stated when discussing the results.


4.9.1 Inventory-Based Price Monitoring

The price-monitoring view presents a modeled price snapshot derived from
stored inventory quantities. It can show crop-level indicators and
estimated movement or pressure based on relative supply information. The
display is a heuristic index, not a currency price quote or a record of
externally sourced market prices.

The price-monitoring function provides users with a relative,

4.9.2 Oversupply and Undersupply Detection

The supply-balance function groups inventory quantities by crop and
compares totals with dynamically calculated thresholds. Depending on the
comparison, the system can classify a crop's current supply as balanced,
undersupply, critical shortage, high supply, or oversupply. Associated
risk alerts provide a short message about the identified supply
condition.

The supply-balance analysis provides an inventory-based indication of

Its output is a classification based on system quantities and thresholds,

feature supports the oversupply and undersupply detection component of
Specific Objective 2.


4.10 Decision-Support Module

The decision-support features analyze stored crop inventory data to
produce crop rankings and demand estimates. The current implementation
uses statistical and rule-based methods. It does not claim to use
artificial intelligence or machine learning.


4.10.1 Crop Recommendation

The Crop Recommendation service ranks crops using a multi-criteria
scoring approach. It defines five criteria: demand strength, supply
stability, seasonal fit, price opportunity, and risk balance. A fixed
pairwise comparison matrix is used to derive criterion weights through
the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP). The system then applies a
Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS)
calculation to score and rank crop options.

The values used in these calculations are derived from the inventory and
forecast information available to the application. They should not be
interpreted as observed buyer demand, verified commodity prices, or
agronomic guarantees. In particular, the criteria labels for demand,
seasonality, and price opportunity are implemented as inventory-derived
proxies; the system does not calculate them from a verified external price
series, an independently observed crop season calendar, or historical
transaction-demand records. The displayed recommendation is decision
support based on the implemented criteria and data, not a promise of
profitability.

Figure 4.14. Crop recommendation interface and results
[INSERT FIGURE 4.14: Screenshot of the implemented crop recommendations
and ranked results.]

Discussion

The crop-recommendation module applies weighted criteria to produce a
ranked output for crops represented in the available data. It supports
Specific Objective 3 by implementing an algorithm-based recommendation
feature. The scope of its conclusion is limited by the inventory-derived
input values and the implemented criteria.


4.10.2 Crop Demand Forecasting

The demand-forecasting service produces a one-step crop quantity estimate
from available positive inventory observations. Where there is sufficient
variation in the available series, the service attempts an ARIMA(1,0,0)
forecast. When there is insufficient data or the model fit fails, the
system uses a linear fallback where possible; for insufficient data it
reports that condition. Forecast quantities are constrained to
non-negative values.

The observations are inventory quantities, not a dedicated historical
transaction-demand dataset. As a result, the current output should be
described as an inventory-derived estimate and not as a validated measure
of future buyer demand. Forecast accuracy must not be claimed until it
has been evaluated against actual observations using an appropriate
method.

4.11 Farmer Reliability Assessment

Agri-Direct displays seller reliability information based on buyer
ratings recorded for eligible marketplace transactions. After a
qualifying completed order, the buyer can submit a one-to-five-star
rating. A seller's displayed score is the average of recorded buyer
ratings, accompanied by a reliability label and rating count. A seller
without ratings is shown as "Not Yet Rated."

Discussion

The rating summary gives a buyer additional information about prior

quality. This feature contributes to the trust and transaction
coordination goals of Specific Objective 1. It is included as an
implemented marketplace feature; it is not presented as a separately
validated scoring model.

4.13 Administrative Module

The Admin Console is an administrator-only workspace. Its main page
provides management cards and summary counts, with links to focused
workspaces:

- Users and roles: search and manage accounts and role information.
- Crop catalog: manage crop and crop-category records used by the system.
- Knowledge Hub: manage articles, drafts, and knowledge categories.
- Inventory: search and review inventory records and available evidence.
- Marketplace: search and manage marketplace listings.
- Audit log: search and filter system activity and export matching
  entries to CSV.

The Admin Console is a navigation and summary page; the detailed records
are handled in the linked workspaces. Administrative access is checked
before the relevant workspace is displayed. The system also provides
administrative review actions for selected geotag submissions.

4.17 Discussion in Relation to the Objectives

Specific Objective 1: Develop a centralized agricultural supply-chain
platform that facilitates product management, transactions, and direct
communication between farmers and buyers.

The Marketplace, listing and inventory functions, purchase and trade
workflows, Messages, and seller-rating displays implement the principal
functions associated with this objective. Users can list products,
browse listings, undertake eligible transactions, and communicate within
the platform. The system uses a shared standard-user role, so the
farmer/buyer distinction is based on activity rather than separate
account modules. Final assessment: [INSERT RELEVANT FUNCTIONAL TEST AND
EVALUATION RESULTS].

Specific Objective 2: Implement market-intelligence modules that monitor
price trends and detect potential oversupply and undersupply conditions.

The supply-balance module classifies inventory-based crop quantities
against dynamic thresholds and reports potential supply signals. The
price-monitoring feature presents a modeled indicator derived from
inventory; it does not retrieve live external commodity prices. The
implemented functions therefore support supply imbalance analysis and
inventory-based price monitoring, subject to the data-source limitation.
Final assessment: [INSERT RELEVANT TEST RESULTS AND STATE THIS LIMITATION].

Specific Objective 3: Develop algorithm-based decision-support features
that provide crop recommendations and demand forecasts.

4.18 Chapter Summary

This chapter presented Agri-Direct as a connected system that begins with
a public landing page and account access, continues through shared
user-facing inventory, Marketplace, transaction, communication, and
information modules, and provides additional management functions to
administrators. The system includes inventory-derived market signals,
multi-criteria crop recommendations, demand quantity estimates, and
buyer-based seller-rating information.

The modules support the first three objectives with important scope
qualifications: the system has shared standard user accounts rather than
separate farmer and buyer roles; price monitoring uses an
inventory-derived index rather than an external live price feed; and
demand forecasting uses inventory observations rather than a dedicated
historical transaction dataset. The fourth objective, formal evaluation,
cannot be concluded until its testing and evaluation data are collected.
The completed results in this chapter should form the evidence base for
the conclusions and recommendations in Chapter V.
